Knock is built in Toronto and works across Canada, as long as two things are true: your building's intercom **calls a phone number**, and you can get a **local phone number** to give it.

## What you need

- **An intercom that dials a phone.** Many apartment and condo buildings in the Toronto area, and across Canada, use a front-door panel that calls a number on file for your unit. Check yours with the [five-minute test](/docs/will-it-work). Wall handsets and vendor apps (video intercoms) won't work.
- **A Canadian phone number to give your building.** Knock uses [Telnyx](/docs/telnyx) for the number. Telnyx sells local numbers in Canada, and Telnyx numbers can only be bought in your account's own country, so a Canadian account gets Canadian numbers (area codes such as 416, 647, 226 and others, depending on what's available).
- **A card.** You'll add about $5 of credit to upgrade the account so it accepts your building's calls. See [costs](/docs/costs). Telnyx bills in US dollars.

## Asking your building to change the number

Your property manager, concierge or building's intercom company controls the number the panel calls. Use the [message template](/docs/forward-apartment-buzzer-to-cell-phone) and ask them to switch your unit to your new Canadian number. In condos, this often goes through the management office or the concierge desk. Some charge a small fee.

## What Knock costs in Canada

The software is free. A Canadian local number is about $1 a month, plus about half a cent a minute for calls, so most households pay roughly $1 to $2 a month. Prices change, so check Telnyx's own number pricing before you buy.

## Notes

- **Backup number.** Knock can ring a backup number if nobody opens the door. Telnyx limits which countries a Paid account can call, so make sure the backup number's country is allowed.
- **Emergency-calling rules.** Telnyx asks for a service address when you upgrade. That is normal for phone numbers.
- **Rules of your building.** Some buildings don't allow remote door release. Check your lease and condo rules.
