Most apartment buzzer apps solve the same problem: your building's intercom calls a phone number, and you want that call to reach your phone, or several roommates' phones, with an easy way to unlock the door. They differ in who runs the service and how you pay.

:::warn What this page is based on
The descriptions of other apps come from their public App Store listings and a web search on the date above. We haven't tested them, and prices and features change. Check each listing before you decide.
:::

## How each one works

| App | How it works | Setup and cost |
|---|---|---|
| **Knock** | Open source. You deploy it, and it answers your building's call on a number you own. Everyone's phone is notified, and the first tap sends the unlock digit. | About 30 minutes. Free software, about $1 to $2 a month for the phone line. |
| **DoorBuzzin** | Forwards the buzzer call to several phones and can open the door from the app. Its listing says it works with any doorbell that calls a phone number. | Install the app. Pricing: see its listing. |
| **Lowkey** | You get a virtual number by subscribing, and send it to your building manager so the buzzer dials it. | Install, then ask your manager. Subscription, per its listing. |
| **BuzzerBee** | A dedicated number that your manager points the buzzer to. You set the digit it presses to unlock (usually 6 or 9). | Install, then ask your manager. Pricing: see its listing. |
| **Building systems** (ButterflyMX, Schonell) | The building installs a video intercom, and you use the vendor's app. | Decided and usually paid for by the building. |

## When Knock is a good fit

- You want **no subscription to a buzzer company**, and you're comfortable paying a phone company about a dollar or two a month.
- You live with **roommates**, and you want everyone's phone to ring and the first person who is home to open the door.
- You like that it is **open source** and runs on **your own accounts**, so your call log and phone number belong to you.

## When something else is better

- You want to **install an app and be done in two minutes**. Knock takes about 30 minutes, mostly creating a Telnyx account. A hosted app is simpler.
- You don't want to look after anything. With Knock, **you** are the operator. If a service it depends on changes, you update it.
- Your building has a **video intercom or a vendor app**. A phone-call-based buzzer app won't work there.

## What every call-based app needs from your building

All of the call-forwarding apps depend on the same thing: your building's intercom **must be able to call a phone number**, and **unlock when you press a digit**. Check yours first with the [five-minute test](/docs/will-it-work), then see how to [get the number changed](/docs/forward-apartment-buzzer-to-cell-phone).
