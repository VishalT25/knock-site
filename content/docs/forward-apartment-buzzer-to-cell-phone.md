---
Many apartment buildings no longer have a handset in the unit. The panel by the front door dials a phone number instead, and you press a digit on your phone to unlock the door. That number is stored in the building's system, so **you can't forward it yourself from your phone's settings. Whoever manages the system changes it.**

## Step 1: find out if your building calls a phone number

Look at the panel by the front door, or ask a neighbor. If your unit is listed by name or code and a visitor can "call" it, the panel is almost certainly dialing a number. Two signs it is not:

- A wall handset or a screen inside your unit that rings. That is a wired intercom, and it won't call a phone.
- A vendor app that your building made you install (a video intercom). It is tied to that vendor's system.

If the panel rings a phone, you are in the right place. The [five-minute check](/docs/will-it-work) walks through the rest.

## Step 2: ask for the number to be changed

The person who can change the number is your property manager, concierge, or the intercom vendor they use. A short message works:

> Hi, I'd like to change the phone number my unit's door intercom calls. The new number is +1 (xxx) xxx-xxxx. Could you update it, and tell me which digit I press to unlock the door? Thanks!

Ask for two things: the new number, and **the digit that unlocks the door** (see [which digit opens the door](/docs/intercom-unlock-digit)). Some buildings only allow a change by email or through a form, and some charge a small fee.

## Step 3: decide whose phone should ring

If you live alone, give them your mobile number and you are done. The catch with roommates is that one number rings one phone. You have three choices:

1. **Give the number of one person.** Simple, but that person has to be home, and everyone else has to ask them.
2. **Use a shared number that rings several phones.** That means a virtual number and some software to ring everyone.
3. **Use Knock.** It answers the call on a number you own, notifies everyone's phone, and presses the unlock digit when the first person taps **Open door**. It is free and open source; you host it yourself. Setup takes about 30 minutes and the phone line costs about $1 to $2 a month. [How it works](/docs/using-knock) and [what it costs](/docs/costs).

## Step 4: test it

Have a friend buzz your unit. Your phone should ring from an unfamiliar number. Answer, press the unlock digit, and the door should click. If the call never arrives, the building may be dialing an old number. Ask again, and see [troubleshooting](/docs/troubleshooting).

## When it can't work

Some systems can't dial out at all, only ring a wired handset. Others only work with a vendor's own app. In those cases, a building-side upgrade is the only route, and that is the property manager's decision.
