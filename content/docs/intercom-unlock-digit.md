When an apartment intercom calls your phone, the door doesn't open by itself. You press a key on your phone's keypad during the call, and the intercom hears that tone and releases the lock. The key is set by the building's system.

## The usual answer

**9** is the most common unlock digit. Some systems use a different one, such as **6**, **5**, or **#**. There is no universal standard, so guessing at the front door is a poor plan if a visitor is waiting.

## How to find yours

1. **Ask your property manager or concierge.** This is the most reliable answer. Add it to your message when you ask them to [update the number the intercom calls](/docs/forward-apartment-buzzer-to-cell-phone).
2. **Check the panel.** Some panels, or the instructions in your move-in package, print it.
3. **Test it.** Ask a friend to buzz you, answer, and try 9 first. If nothing happens, try the other candidates one at a time, and note which one clicks the door.

## What the tone is, and why a phone app can send it

Pressing a key on a call sends a short audio tone called **DTMF**, the same signal used for "press 1 for sales." Any phone, and any service that handles calls, can send it. That is how Knock opens the door: it answers the call, waits for someone to tap **Open door**, and sends your digit as a DTMF tone.

## Setting the digit in Knock

Knock's default is **9**. If your building uses another digit, change it in Knock's settings under **Unlock digit**. See [Settings](/docs/settings). The test call in [first-time setup](/docs/first-run) lets you confirm the tone is sent.

## If no digit works

Some intercoms only call to talk and have no unlock feature over the phone, and others unlock through a vendor's own app. Check with your building. If your system can't unlock from a call, a phone-based buzzer app can't help, and the [intercom check](/docs/will-it-work) will tell you before you spend anything.
