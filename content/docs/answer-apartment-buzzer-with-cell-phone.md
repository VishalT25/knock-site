Many apartment buildings have no handset in the unit. When a visitor taps your name or unit code at the front door, the panel dials a regular phone number, and you answer it like any other call.

## What happens, step by step

1. The visitor enters your unit code or picks your name on the panel.
2. The panel calls the phone number your building has on file for you.
3. Your phone rings, usually from a number you don't recognize. Answer it. You'll hear the visitor, or silence on some systems.
4. To let them in, **press the unlock digit** on your keypad during the call. It is usually **9**, but not always. See [which digit opens the door](/docs/intercom-unlock-digit).
5. The door releases for a few seconds. Hang up.

If you don't want them in, just hang up.

## Make sure you don't miss the call

- Save the number as a contact (for example "Front door") after the first call, so it can ring through Focus and Do Not Disturb.
- Check that your phone isn't set to silence unknown callers. If calls aren't arriving, see [apartment buzzer not calling your phone](/docs/apartment-buzzer-not-calling-my-phone).
- Keep your number on file up to date with your property manager if you change phones.

## Roommates: one number, one phone

The panel calls one number. If you live with other people, that is the catch: whoever's phone is on file gets the call, and everyone else has to ask them. Ways around it:

- **Take turns.** Whoever expects a visitor gets the number changed. Slow, and building managers often charge or push back.
- **Use a shared number that rings everyone.** That needs a virtual number and software to ring several phones.
- **Use Knock.** It answers the building's call on a number you own and rings every roommate's phone. The first person to tap **Open door** lets the visitor in. It is free, open source, and runs on your own accounts. See [how it works](/docs/using-knock) and [what it costs](/docs/costs).

## Will it work in your building?

This works if your panel dials a phone number and unlocks when you press a digit. It doesn't work with wall handsets or vendor-app-only intercoms. Check yours in five minutes with the [intercom test](/docs/will-it-work).
